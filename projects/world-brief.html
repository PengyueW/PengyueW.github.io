World Brief

Local-first world news

Fifteen minutes a day instead of browsing news sites. ~700 feeds from all 197 nations, grouped into events, placed on a map, ranked, and translated entirely on your own machine. No AI service, no API key, no server of mine anywhere in the loop.

23,000 articles in 15 minutes.

With information so accessible today I discovered that somehow my attention is so fragmented I actually read less proper news than before, and I feel that apart from global hotspots I am in reality hearing less from what is going on in other parts of the world. Therefore I created this project to spend a little time every day to gain a comprehensive view of what is happening everywhere, to hear the different sides of the story, and to processs the events from different outlets in different languages with ease. The app incorporates learning models that train themselves on the corpus they just fetched. Every translation runs offline on your CPU. Nothing you read leaves the machine.

🗞️ ~500 labelled outlets 🌐 197 nations covered 🗺️ Events on a world map 🈯 16 interface languages 🔌 Fully offline translation 🔑 No API key
Note: Since the app is not published on the Mac App Store, opening it the first time may prompt a security warning. To solve this issue, open System Preferences -> Privacy & Security, and give it permission to install.
What it does

A newsroom that fits in a laptop

A brief is not a feed. Articles are clustered into events, each event is ranked on how widely and how independently it is being covered, and the day's selection is packed greedily into a reading budget, with at least two independent outlets required before anything makes the cut.

📋
The brief
Ranked events with time, place, relevance and reading time; a neutral extractive summary where every sentence names the outlet that published it, and a timer tracking the 15-minute daily budget.
🗺️
World map
Events as markers: colour for macro-topic, size for relevance, a ring for "in today's brief", plus country hotspots and dashed links between nations the same stories tie together. Leaflet and Natural Earth outlines, both vendored: no tile server, no key.
⚖️
Sides of the story
Articles grouped by the outlets' country or by editorial polarity: left, right, centre, state-government, independent-opposition, with the most central headline per side and a framing-divergence score. State and party media are included deliberately, and marked.
🧭
Places without a geocoder
A gazetteer of 197 countries with demonyms and exonyms in ten languages plus ~270 cities and hotspots, matched by token n-grams in one pass, with substring matching for Chinese, Japanese and Korean and guards against person names and overshadowed countries.
🧠
Models it trains itself
Keyword rules as weak supervision plus 220 seed headlines train 22 one-vs-rest logistic classifiers over hashed word and character n-grams, retrained on the live corpus at every refresh. A salience regressor learns newsworthiness from the app's own clustering and your feedback.
🔤
Offline translation
Argos/OpenNMT weights run by CTranslate2: downloaded once, then used forever offline, pivoting through English where no direct pair exists. A SQLite translation memory means a headline costs ~20 ms once and nothing after.
Under the hood

The pipeline, stage by stage

FetchAsync httpx + feedparser, 24 concurrent, per-feed health recorded
StoreSQLite, 14-day retention, per-article feature cache invalidated by a version stamp
EventsTF-IDF cosine agglomeration per language, centroid check against chaining, cross-language merge on shared places
RankingCoverage breadth, category gravity, tier-1 bonus, recency decay, predicted salience, contestedness, your feedback
SummaryUnsupervised extractive: sentence centroid relevance with MMR diversity, attributed per sentence
API / UIFastAPI JSON API; vanilla JS and Leaflet, no framework, no CDN
Desktop shellOne process: uvicorn on a loopback port, the refresh scheduler, and a native WKWebView / WebView2 / WebKitGTK window over it
PackagingPyInstaller for three desktop systems with native installers; a Kotlin client for Android; every icon drawn by make_icons.py
Full refresh~100 s for 700 feeds and 23,000 articles on an Apple-silicon Mac: 3 s of that clustering, ~5 s training and classification
Downloads

Every platform

One click on the icon starts everything: server, refresh scheduler and window are a single process, and closing the window leaves the news being collected in the background. The Windows and Linux installers are built from the same tree with packaging/build.sh.

🤝 Built with Claude, directed by me

I used Claude as an implementation assistant throughout: boilerplate generation, unit-test scaffolding, and accelerated front-end visuals. The engineering judgement is mine.

  • I conceptualised the architecture, the pipeline stages, the local-first constraint, and the decision that no external AI service or API key would ever be involved.
  • I peer-reviewed the code that came back, and rejected or reworked what did not fit the design.
  • I debugged the inconsistencies and functional flaws, clustering that chained across unrelated stories, gazetteer false positives, translation pivots, packaging failures.
  • I guided Claude throughout, deciding what to build next and how each piece should behave.
