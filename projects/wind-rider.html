Wind Rider

A flying game for the browser. Put on a pair of wings, dive through crystal gates and skim the rocks across nine floating worlds, alone, against your own ghost, or in a room with up to twelve friends.

Who made it

Six people, one game.

Wind Rider isn't a solo project. Six of us started it over a weekend at the VIScon hackathon under the team name It Works On My Machine, with one rule we kept repeating to each other: it should look and feel like gliding in Sky: Children of the Light or Genshin Impact, but play like a racer. The hackathon is over and we are still working on it.

Three ways to fly

Pick a mode

Every course comes from a seed. The same seed gives everyone the same islands, tunnels and gates, which is what makes racing, ghosts and shared leaderboards possible.

🏁

Race friends

Create a room, send the code, and everyone flies the same map at the same time. You see the other pilots live, with their capes and trails, and the lobby can switch maps between races.

⏱️

Time trial

Fly today's course, or any of the nine maps, against the clock and the ghosts of the fastest runs. Each seed keeps its own leaderboard.

🌀

Adventure

A roguelike run through eight different worlds joined by portals. At every portal you choose one of three upgrades, so no two runs fly the same. Three feathers, and a crash costs one.

What's in it

More than a weekend's worth

🌍
Nine worlds
Sky Isles, Verdant Highlands, Glacier Peaks, Sky Archipelago, Coral Lagoon, Twilight Forest, Ember Wastes, Aurora Summit and the Sunken Reef. Each has its own landforms, plants, buildings, music, and a day and a night mood.
🪶
A flight model that rewards nerve
The nose points where you go and gravity trades height for speed. Diving makes you fast, and skimming close to rock earns style points and a boost. On top of that you get a held boost, an air brake and a quick sideways dodge.
✨
Twenty abilities, twenty items
For the adventure: a spirit manta to draft behind, a wind tunnel that widens the gates ahead, a song that turns gates to face you, falcon and butterfly stances, a firework strapped to your back, and plenty more.
🎒
Your pilot, your look
Crystals and treasures you collect on runs level up a profile and unlock capes, hats, boots, decorated wings and eight pets that follow you through the gates.
👻
Ghosts and leaderboards
Finished runs are recorded at 15 Hz and stored with their time, so you race the translucent ghosts of the best pilots on the same seed.
📱
Keyboard, gamepad or phone
Mouse and keyboard with rebindable keys, a gamepad, or touch: on a phone held sideways, a stick appears under your thumb, with buttons for boost, brake and abilities. New players get a short tutorial flight if they want one.
Under the hood

How it's built

FrontendReact 19, TypeScript and Vite; the game itself is plain Three.js, outside React
LookCel-shaded toon materials with ink outlines, HDR bloom, a painted sky shader, one GPU particle system for every effect
TerrainSigned distance fields meshed with surface nets in Web Workers, streamed in an LOD octree, land fading in out of the haze
CollisionsA body sphere and two wing rectangles tested against the exact triangles on screen, so what you hit is what you see
SimulationFixed 120 Hz physics steps, rendering interpolated between them; every course deterministic from its seed
MultiplayerFastAPI rooms relaying pilot positions over Server-Sent Events at 15 Hz
PersistenceSQLite for leaderboards, ghosts and adventure profiles; server-checked finish times
DeploymentDocker Compose, with Caddy serving the build and proxying the API
Performance target60 fps on an integrated laptop GPU at High quality, with automatic quality steps below that
It isn't online for the public yet. The game runs fully in the browser, but rooms, profiles and leaderboards need the backend, so for now it is played locally or on the hackathon VM. Putting it up for everyone is one of the next things on our list.

🤝 Six people, one codebase

Six people changing one game at the same time breaks things fast, so we kept a few habits:

  • Changes come in as pull requests onto main, rebased rather than merged, so the history stays readable.
  • The race map is frozen and checked by hashes, so leaderboards and ghosts never break when someone else's work lands.
  • Looks never change gameplay. Visual work can't touch the flight model, collisions or timing.
  • A shared design document describes the art direction, the architecture and a dated log of every change, so anyone can pick up where someone else stopped.
